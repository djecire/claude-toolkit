# Craft study log: mashups, wordplay, mixing, effects (started 25/09/2026)

Why: Evan's verdict on my first MCP mashup was "a car crash": two vocals at once and an acapella

## C01 Chris Deluxe, "ULTIMATE guide for creating your own MASHUP!" (kmcS7otxSnA, 17/05/2023, 19:17)
Radio 538 mashup producer; Ableton.
- Choosing: same key (Mixed In Key / rekordbox / Serato), "somewhat acceptable" tempo range; audition
  combos live on decks first (FLX10 real-time stem filter) before any editing.
- Sources, best to worst: full stems > official instrumental + acapella > separated. Acapellas4u, label/
  management requests.
- Headroom: pull every channel to about -6 dB before starting (mastered material; two tracks at once
  clip the Main).
- Warping: first kick > warp from here (straight) > metronome check through the whole song > fix drift
  late in the song with a Shift-dragged warp marker. Acapella has no kick: warp it against the original
  song (start marker on the first word, line the acapella up under the original's vocal, fix drift where
  the waveforms match).
- Tempo: set = the slowest song (122), then automate the Song Tempo up to the next song's 126 over
  about 40 s. (Range fields on the Main tempo automation.)
- Arrangement: let the first song play its chorus, trim repeats (cut final chorus, move it to the first
  chorus spot, Cmd+E), use a song ending that fades as room for the transition; start the acapella with
  8 counts of pause before the transition "to create tension"; blend the acapella into the other song's
  BREAK / BUILD-UP so everything lands together.
- Wordplay inside a mashup: Levels' Etta James "oh" and a similar sounding "oh" in the other song ->
  copy that phrase to its own track, place it on the grid, add Reverb with dry/wet automated up (build),
  then an automated filter to fade it away.
- FX: sweeps/risers from sample packs at very low volume ("you barely notice them... you miss them when
  off"); a sweep flowing across both songs binds the transition.
- Glue: gentle compressor on the Main (just touching), so vocals sit "in" the track, not on top.
- Export whole length incl. Main; loudness match with Platinum Notes (Evan owns it).

## C08 DJ Blakey (DMC champion), "How DJs Know EXACTLY When To Mix (Phrasing Explained)" (1dLfSkKLhuk, 14/05/2026, 24:22)
- Beatmatched but out of phase = wrong: one track drops while the other is still in its breakdown ->
  "mismatch in the energy"; the floor doesn't know what to do.
- Dance music is built in sections (intro, breakdown, drop, breakdown, drop, outro), designed to be
  mixed: intro = time to align, breakdown = breathe, drop = release.
- A phrase = 32 beats = 8 bars; at the end of every phrase something changes (hats/percussion in,
  vocal in/out, breakdown starts, drop). Press play on the incoming track AT THE END OF A 32-BEAT PHRASE.
- Energy alignment: breakdown-to-breakdown mixing -> both drop together ("double drop"). Good phrasing
  = sections line up, not just beats.
- Hip-hop: intro, verse, chorus, verse, chorus...; chorus 16 or 32 beats, less rigid; mix in from the
  chorus and have the old track OUT before its next verse. Know the music.
- Hot cues named and coloured per section (his: 1 intro/first kick, 4 breakdown, 5 first drop).
- Beat jump +/-32 beats keeps phrase alignment; use it live to delay a drop/breakdown so sections align
  (Ableton equivalent: move/duplicate by whole 8-bar blocks).

## C09 DJ Shortee, "How to Count Phrases & Identify the Main ONE Beat in Each Phrase" (kpsvKJ_6bsc, 30/04/2023, 19:17)
- Beat = letter, bar = word, phrase = sentence. Phrase length varies by genre and section:
  hip-hop/rock/pop/vocal music 2, 4, 8 or 16 bars; house/techno/trance/DnB 8, 16, 32 or 64 bars.
- "The one" = first beat of a phrase = where you drop the next song.
- Clues: small (extra drum fill) every 4 bars, bigger (crash) every 8, huge (new instrument, drum roll,
  big crash, fill before the drop) every 16 or 32. Small clues mid-phrase, bigger clues at the end.
- Real example (house): 16-bar intro (two 8s), breakdown of 24 = 16 + an 8-bar BRIDGE into the drop,
  drop 16. So sections are not always powers of two: count in 8s, find the biggest change.
- Vocal music: verse typically 16 bars, chorus typically 8 (sometimes 4), occasionally 32. Shorter
  phrases -> shorter mixes than EDM. Waveform shape shows section lengths once one is counted.

## C10 Chris M, "Phrase Mixing: Remove Bad DJ Transitions Forever" (856bRfd_4S4, 31/10/2022, 6:26)
(Evan already uses Chris M's six roles in his library.)
- Beat > bar (4 beats, the downbeat may carry a crash/hat) > phrase, commonly 8 bars / 32 beats (6 and 4
  also occur). Phrases differ musically (new vocal, instrument, chord change); a clue ends each phrase
  (drum roll, riser, everything pulled back for tension).
- Structure: intro (gradual drums/percussion build), verses, breakdown (strip back, build tension),
  chorus/drop, bridge (musically different, links sections, more common with vocals), outro (mirror of
  the intro, stripped, losing energy).
- Mixes enabled by phrasing: intro over outro (first one to learn), double drop (two choruses/drops
  together, DnB), chopping in different sections at different times.

## C04 DJ Phil Harris, "The REAL Secret To Perfect DJ Acapellas!" (CcXlgN_jFg0, 26/02/2021, 10:06)
- Keep the ORIGINAL song next to every acapella. Key: take the original's key (analysis tools guess
  wrong on acapellas) and write it onto the acapella.
- Speed: YouTube uploads are often sped/pitched to dodge takedowns. Play acapella and original from the
  same word side by side; if they drift, re-warp or scrap it. (In Ableton: warp the acapella against the
  original; Chris Deluxe's method.)
- Grid: copy the original's grid (and exact BPM, e.g. 122.88) onto the acapella, bar markers aligned,
  metronome check.

## C07 DJ Trayze (Beat Refinery), "Incorporating Word Play In Your Live Mixes" (_w7ED8EedNY, 03/11/2015, 4:25)
Wordplay = the link between two songs is a word or phrase; works across genres and energy levels.
1. Word-to-word: Red Hot Chili Peppers "Around the World" (from the chorus into the first verse) ->
   loop the verse bit that says "California" -> drop Dr Dre/2Pac "California Love" on the one right
   after (its "California" answers). Rock -> hip-hop.
2. Name-drop: Biggie "Dreams" loop "Mariah... kind of scary" -> echo out -> Mariah Carey. The lyric
   introduces the next artist. Hip-hop -> R&B, energy down on purpose.
3. Name-drop: LL Cool J "Phenomenon" loop "she love Tupac..." -> 2Pac "I Get Around". Keeps party energy
   high; quick, fast, fun.
Mechanics: loop a short section holding the word (1 or 2 bars) so it repeats as a call, then cut or echo
out on the beat and drop the next song's one (or its matching word).

## C11 Club Ready DJ School (Andrew), "7 DJ FX to Enhance your Transitions" (fe652rGDaFE, 01/03/2021, 12:28)
- EQ work is the cake, effects are the icing: get the EQ blend right first, then add FX on top.
- FX go on the EXITING track, to "say goodbye" while the new one comes in.
- The seven goodbyes: clean EQ "round out" (no FX); 1-beat echo out ("cheerful"; common, but not every
  track); spinback (sometimes with a bit of echo); trans (rhythmic gate, 1 beat, intensify); noise
  (sweep on the exiting song, "wipe out"; also a gap filler for build-ups/down bits); low-pass filter
  crunch on the exiting song (techno); vinyl brake (slow release) to stop the old song dead.
- Vary them: a clean mix is the default; effects occasionally.

## C12 Crossfader, "I Went from BORING to EPIC DJ Transitions Using These Techniques!" (Lk0a6U6m2Zg, 17/10/2024, 15:52)
1. DROP SWAP: same BPM, same key (or key sync). Cue both tracks exactly 32 beats before their drops,
   start the incoming one when the playing one reaches its cue: the crowd expects drop A, gets drop B.
   Count in 4s; crossfader cut on the one.
2. BIG TEMPO JUMP (130 -> 174): loop an isolated vocal bit of the outgoing track, add trans FX, raise
   the tempo during the loop, then drop the new track from a cue 4 beats before its drop. Works for
   hip-hop <-> house, trap <-> DnB.
3. WORDPLAY on house: from an acapella (Salt-N-Pepa "Push It": "push it... watch me work it"), search
   the library for the same words ("work it" -> Missy Elliott "Work It" bootleg). Hot cues on the
   words, finger-drum patterns on the one ("watch me, watch me, work it"), then mix into the track.
   Practise the pattern over just a beat first.
4. KEY-SHIFT RISER: 4-beat loop of the outgoing vocal, step the key up in semitones for tension, add a
   touch of echo at the end, return to the original key, drop the next track on the one.

## C13 Crossfader (Louis John), "How to make a DJ Edit in 30 minutes" (Hc7f_mvKeA8, 20/10/2025, 29:51)
Goal of an edit: keep the magic of the original, give it "chunk" for the floor (his original track
got a "really?" from a club crowd).
- Stems from a splitter; original + all stems aligned to the first kick at 1.1.1; key/BPM from
  rekordbox (120, G minor); speed up modestly (120 -> 125), Complex warp so everything follows.
- Replace the original drums with club drums (kick loop, clap, hats, two percussion loops panned
  slightly L/R), keep the original bassline (layered with a sub following the same notes, EQ high-pass
  at ~50-60 Hz, sidechain to the kick: 4:1, attack ~0.1 ms, release ~50 ms), keep the synths
  (sidechained the same way). Original drums kept high-passed for their fills (edit still feels like
  the original).
- Arrangement: Insert Silence 64 bars at the start; DJ INTRO = 16 bars (~30 s) of drums (+ bass later);
  extend the short original intro into a proper BUILD (duplicate the vocal hook, filter it in, keep
  tops, leave a low-end gap "ba-bam 3 4 5 6 7"), then a riser (fix its warp tempo so it fills exactly),
  a snare roll across the whole build, a crash on the one of the drop. Each element added raises the
  build's energy "from there to there". Leave room for the vocal to breathe.
- Keep an odd bar if it's the track's character, or edit it out for a DJ-friendly grid.
- Colour code: drums red, bass brown, instruments blue, vocals black, FX white.

## C14 Laidback Luke, "#305 Production Tutorial: How To Make Extended Versions" (g0yhzhkeOFo, 18/06/2023, 25:47)
Why: extended versions give DJs mix-in / mix-out sections; late-90s vinyl had 1-1.5 min beat intros
and outros (32 bars); now 16-bar intros/outros are the norm, 8 bars is enough for fast festival mixing.
- OUTRO FIRST (then reuse its beats for the intro). Take a busy 4-bar section, loop to 16 bars.
  Remove vocals and most melodic elements; keep a hint (filtered bleep, chords for the first 8 bars)
  so it still sounds like the track. Fade elements out (bass gain automated to 0 over the outro, a
  Utility fade on the harsh synth). Keep the bass in ONE key to the end: don't confuse DJs with a key
  change in the outro. Last 8 bars: EQ the low end out of the bass and even the kick ("as if the DJ
  is EQing"), optionally fade the kick; finish with a reverse cymbal + fill.
- Watch loop artefacts: a 4-bar copy repeats its crash every 4 bars and its first-bar kick pickup;
  fix so crashes land every 8 bars.
- INTRO is not a copy of the outro: the opposite dynamic, filter/fade IN. 16 bars of space
  (Cmd+I insert). Don't reveal the bassline (save it for the drop). First 8: beats + a quiet hint
  (bleep at low volume), low end EQ'd out of the kick; second 8: more percussion, chords fade in, open
  hats; steal the outro's FX (reverse cymbal + fill) right before the track proper begins.
- Boring beats are the point: DJs will lay them over another track's drop or chorus.
- Use only existing elements of the track. Variants: 8-bar intro (cut the middle 8, Cmd+Shift+X),
  16-bar outro or vice versa.
- Render trick: an empty MIDI clip named "Extended" spanning first kick to last tail = one-click
  export range.

## C02 DJ Mag with David Guetta, "How to make remixes & mash-ups" (kUoI5Vv8Bu8, 01/07/2021, 17:58; Whisper transcript)
- Sets are 100% his own edits/mashups so the crowd hears something unavailable anywhere else; weeks
  of prep per set.
- Vocal first: key and tempo from Mixed In Key (5A, 123). "The song doesn't start on the one... I can
  hear the one is on [the word], so I flag it": find the real downbeat by ear, not the file start.
- Use only the HOOK ("no verses, nothing") when the song's chorus is the strong part.
- Mashup: "introduce those vocals in a way people are not like 'hmm what is this?'": pair the new
  vocal with a record the crowd recognises instantly (his One Love Takes Over piano; "people scream
  immediately"). Transposed the INSTRUMENTAL 3 semitones up to match the vocal's key (Complex).
  Kept the bass pedalling on one note for tension. Then drop into a record in the relative minor
  (A major <-> F# minor share notes), transposing the drop 1 semitone rather than changing key mid-
  mashup. Structure: familiar -> new vocal -> surprise banger drop.
- Mix: iZotope Neutron to find masking; cut the instrumental around 3 kHz for the vocal; vocal on its
  own track, sidechain-compressed to the beat once drums come in; delay send also sidechained.
- Transitions/FX last: an intro to mix in, impact/"explode" into the breakdown, a lift.

## C03 Club Ready DJ School, "How to Mix Vocals - Avoid Vocals on Vocals" (sbuNltLIP7A, 23/09/2019, 15:21; Whisper transcript)
- Vocals on vocals is "generally a no": too busy, a mess.
- Usual entry: after the (or over the end of the) chorus; don't step on the chorus, it's the main part.
- If the outgoing song goes straight back into a verse, you must have it OUT before that verse: EQ the
  new song in underneath early, swap early, roll the old one out -> mix quicker than with house.
- Party/pop: same rule; the dance-music habit of swapping basses at the end of the chorus leaves the
  next verse clashing.
- Use the MIDS to roll out a song ("the mids are the song's personality"), not only the bass.

## C06 Off The Rack Jack, "2 Must-Know DJ Wordplay Mixing Techniques" (PGutKpzgD8o, 22/02/2025, 9:19; Whisper)
- Method 1, LOOP FIRST (more impact, builds anticipation): loop the word/phrase on the outgoing track
  (2-beat loop covers "I need you" / "please stand up"), let it repeat a few times, bring the next
  track in at FULL volume on its matching words, then flick channels every 2 beats so the phrases
  bounce off each other ("I need you" / "I need you"), then stay on the new track.
- Method 2, MIX FIRST (subtle): normal mix, bring B's intro in (here at the end of A's chorus as A's
  verse starts, because the word is in that verse), then loop the word in A ("California", 1-beat loop
  for a single short word like in "Move Bitch" -> "Paint the Town Red") and hand over on it. The crowd
  hears a normal mix until they catch the wordplay.
- Intro versions (record pools) make the incoming side easy.

## D01 Club Ready DJ School, "How to Mix Different Genres & BPMs: 5 Simple Tips" (c24K8AKZ2Ok, 12/02/2025, 17:50)
Principle: play the right song for the moment regardless of genre/BPM; "track selection is king".
1. Mix into a BREAK (a gap with no percussion): any BPM can start there (he jumped ~30 BPM). Either keep
   the beat going (play B on the first beat after the break) or an IMPACT mix: let it drop off, then bang
   in with something instantly recognisable.
2. No break? Make one: fade the old song out over the last couple of bars of the CHORUS (never cut a
   chorus in half; "respect the chorus") and hit play on the new song, or wait for the phrase end.
   Filter the exiting song if a plain fade feels thin.
3. Echo on the exiting track over the last bars of the chorus (he used 3/4-beat echo).
4. ±5 BPM rule: meet in the middle (90 & 100 -> 95), then drift back to original speed afterwards.
5. Big gaps (20-50 BPM): sync, blend, and move the master tempo PART of the way during the
   transition (120 -> 107 for a 100 BPM song), finish the drift afterwards. Or pitch the exiting song down
   to a halt (key lock off, wide range) at the end of a chorus, then hit play: a statement, once or twice
   a set, into something big.
- Play to the room, not for yourself: an R&B/hip-hop crowd at a party doesn't want a techno lesson.

## D02 Crossfader, "How To Transition EASILY Between Different BPMs" (nrpp5AU5kSA, 08/06/2022, 9:00)
Bass house (128) -> hardstyle (150): relevant to Evan's hard-dance weekend.
- Slow the hardstyle track to 128 (tempo range ±16 %), KEY LOCK OFF (it pitches down; speeding it back
  up pitches it up = extra build-up energy).
- Skip its vocal part (sounds drawn out slowed); set a hot cue at the big synth BUILD; count the build:
  8 bars before vocal returns = the window.
- Mix the start in normally with EQ, then instead of waiting, isolate a last bar, jump to the build hot
  cue and ramp the tempo up to 150 across exactly those 8 bars (pace it to finish on time), then key lock
  back on. In Ableton: Song Tempo automation 128 -> 150 over the 8-bar build, the hardstyle clip in
  Re-Pitch or with transpose automation for the pitch-rise feel.

## D03 DJ Blakey, "How to Flawlessly Mix Any Genre in a DJ Set" (PH8G7aV3lUw, 19/03/2025, 15:08)
A 3-minute hip-hop -> house -> DnB routine, built from scratch:
- Crate from a chart/playlist; look at each waveform for breakdowns and long drum sections; sort by key
  to find mashup candidates (1A pairs with 12A, 1A, 2A, 1B).
- LYRIC-LINK bridge: Missy Elliott "Work It" vocal is re-sung in Skrillex "Ratata" (feat. Missy):
  isolate the vocal with stems, play it off hot cues while syncing and raising tempo 102 -> 124, drop
  Ratata from a cue just before its last drop. Wide tempo range (±50 %) needed.
- Mashup moment: Duke Dumont "Ocean Drive" vocal (stems) over Kaytranada? "Move" instrumental from its
  breakdown hot cue; check the separated instrumental is clean first ("stems can produce poor
  results"); simple baby scratches to introduce the acapella; a chilled middle section.
- Use only the part of a song you love (James Hype "Lose Control"'s SWV sample section), then set a
  1-BEAT LOOP on its vocal and mix the DnB track under it; energy peaks at the end.
- A set/routine must ebb and flow: impactful intro (tempo rise), chilled mashup middle, peak end.
- Routines are performances: practise ~1 hour until you can't get it wrong.

## D04 Digital DJ Tips (Phil Morse), "How to mix ANYTHING with this one DJ transition [Open-format]" (RnewIvHC45o, 01/12/2025, 5:43)
- Mobile/wedding/party DJs must jump genres, BPMs and decades (EDM -> 80s hip-hop -> oldies).
- ECHO OUT: a 1-beat echo (half-beat is also popular) = the track stops but its rhythm carries on and
  fades, giving a few seconds; then start the new track at a PRE-CHOSEN point that lands well (e.g. the
  first vocal line of a verse everyone knows). No beatmix needed; works for any BPM/genre/style.
- Preparation is the magic: cue points set, parts known, tracks beat-gridded so the echo is in time.
- "People just want to dance"; what they notice is poor transitions or lost energy.
- Could be used after every track in an open-format set; people are used to it.

## D06 DJ Blakey, "The Only 5 Transitions You Need as a Beginner DJ" (my9n3W3uJDE, 07/05/2026, 18:44)
Focus on less; each transition has a purpose. Start B at the end of a 32-beat phrase.
1. LONG BLEND: volume faders only, very gradual swap; works almost anywhere; teaches control.
2. BASS SWAP: both at full volume, B's bass off, gradually trade the low EQ; stops mud/distortion.
3. DROP MIX: play A through its breakdown; at the end of the 32-beat phrase, pull A down and start B
   from ITS drop; the crowd expects A's drop and gets B's (use a higher-energy track to lift). Phrasing
   must be exact. Works in hip-hop, DnB, tech house, garage.
4. ECHO OUT: echo on A, fader down, tail rings, start B; tolerates up to ~10 BPM difference. 1-beat echo
   = longer tail = cleaner than 1/2 beat. Example: A's drop +32 beats -> echo out -> B from its
   BREAKDOWN (energy management). Don't overuse: constant echo-outs make sets dull.
5. CROSSFADER CUT: both from the drop, full volume, EQs flat, cut rhythmically between them to make a
   live remix; for high-energy moments.

## D07 DJ Carlo, "10 MUST KNOW DJ Mixing Techniques (Beginner to Pro)" (liu-WYs6kG4, 26/08/2024, 17:15)
- Top DJs rely on simplicity; song selection over everything.
- Default blend: EQs flat, start B at A's "bass end point" (where A's main section ends), lower A's LOW EQ
  while raising B's volume, pull A out at its mix-out point. Add filter/echo for colour.
- BUILD-UP LOOP: 4-beat loop on A, tighten (4 -> 2 -> 1 -> smaller) while B plays, with rising HPF and
  echo. Loop HARMONIC or VOCAL sections only, never "just anything" (James Hype style). Don't do it
  repeatedly: not everyone likes it; smooth mixing matters more.
- Stem mashups live: (1) B's build-up with its vocal isolated over A's chorus with A's vocal isolated/
  muted, then stems of A out gradually; (2) B's intro under A's chorus. Use high-quality files (WAV/
  pool downloads): bad MP3s make worse stems. Extended intros/outros are what make mixing possible.
- EQ blends over 32/64/128-beat increments (isolator EQ mode), so they must be IN KEY (Camelot "T":
  same, ±1, A/B).
- Three levels: (1) B enters with lows off, mids/highs partly down; swap highs at a phrase, quick bass swap
  at the next phrase, then mids, then A's fader out. (2) Same plus a gentle HPF on A while its lows go,
  bass swap on the phrase. (3) Same plus reverb on the exiting track rising with the filter.
- Mixing "anywhere" without section knowledge = the car crash.

## D08 Crossfader (Jimmy Hart), "MIXING IN KEY: How to use HARMONIC MIXING" (U_tS7iMwU54, 19/07/2018, 6:15)
- Key = the set of notes a song sticks to; C major and A minor share all notes (relative); G major and F
  major share all but one note with C major (the neighbours = ±1 on the Camelot wheel). C major = 8B.
- Rules: same code; same number other letter (8B <-> 8A); same letter ±1 (8B -> 7B / 9B).
- Should you always mix in key? Drum loop over drum loop rarely clashes (little melody); clashes come
  when basslines/vocals/melodies overlap. Out of key: mix on the drums-only intro and get out before the
  melodic parts. Plan sets/routines in key; live, if the crowd needs that track next, play it and choose
  the transition (echo out and drop). Key as a creative tool, not a strict rule.

## D12 groboclone, "How to make a mashup in FL Studio (the basics)" (4RPFf8p3wxw, 16/01/2019, 12:31)
(Evan's FL background: the same steps he'd know.)
- Find the acapella (search "acapella", "stems", "multitrack"; prefer higher-fidelity sources).
- Tempo: tap tempo / look up; set project tempo to the INSTRUMENTAL; line the instrumental's bar-1 kick
  up to the grid, metronome check. Tell FL the vocal's tempo, use Stretch mode (not Resample) so pitch
  stays put. (Ableton: warp both; Complex Pro.)
- Key: look it up (e.g. tunebat-type sites) or work it out; compare major with major (or use the
  relative); count semitones on the piano roll; e.g. vocal in G over instrumental in F -> down 200 cents.
- Rhythm: even in key, phrases sit wrong: split the vocal into phrases, make each unique, move/stretch
  so the stressed word ("do", "bug") lands where you'd tap your chest on the beat; slice single notes
  if needed. Intuition + the grid.
- Blend: dry acapella doesn't sit: route all vocal clips to one mixer track, add reverb (large hall to
  taste), EQ out boom.

## D13 EDM Tips (Will), "7 Transition Secrets the Pros Use" (veMIOXVSJZU, 20/05/2022, 30:03)
Production transitions between sections (build -> drop etc.), all in Ableton; pick effects that suit
the genre (check reference tracks). Useful for mashup/edit builds.
1. REVERSE PERCUSSION: big snare+crash with reverb on the one; a reversed copy (transient removed,
   widened with a stereo imager) leading into it.
2. RISERS: Shepard tone or sweep samples rising in pitch across the build; layer a short sweep fading
   in (clip fade), Complex Pro to stretch it to fit, ending on the snare hit; a tiny pause before the drop.
3. REVERSE REVERB ("boss level") on a vocal: copy the first TONAL syllable (the vowel, not the
   consonant), consolidate, Echo (1/4 or 1/8 ping-pong, feedback) + long Reverb, resample the tail to a
   new track, reverse it, trim the original transient, clip-fade into the real vocal; optional Auto Pan
   with its rate automated faster at the end = a spin-up. Introduces the vocal's tone before it arrives.
4. SPATIAL WASHOUT: Reverb as an INSERT on the drums (not a send), dry/wet automated up approaching
   the transition, back to dry on the one: removes transient energy -> bigger contrast when the drop hits.
5. PITCH BEND: 1/16 snare roll with rising velocity and a pitch bend up (±12 st range) near the end;
   short versions as ear candy mid-track; pitch-bend the bass up into a drop (genre-dependent).
6. FILTER AUTOMATION: EQ/filter the low end out of the bass bus or a kick fill just before the
   transition, back to full on the drop; add a reversed kick ending just before the next kick.
7. HINT THE HOOK: play the chorus melody/vocal quietly in the build (short reverb insert, low cut, Auto
   Filter bringing highs in, ping-pong echo, auto pan), switched off exactly at the drop; pop uses this so
   people subconsciously expect the hook.

## D15 DJ TechTools (Ryan Dejaegher), "How to Make a DJ Intro Edit With Ableton Live" (e9BVY676Fi4, 02/06/2014, 11:40)
Intro edit = extended beat/instrumental intro (typically 32 beats = 8 bars) that breaks into the first
verse or chorus. Three ways (Live 9 then, same steps in 12):
1. With the instrumental: cut its first 8 bars (Cmd+E), put them in front of the original cut at the
   start of the verse, on a new "edit" track.
2. No instrumental: find a vocal-free drums-only bar in the original (bar 2 here), loop and duplicate it
   to 8 bars (Cmd+D), swap in a nicer last-beat element, REVERSE the last beat of bar 4 and bar 8 as fills,
   then the original from the verse.
3. Acapella intro (chorus-first): Live mis-detects acapella tempo (81 vs real 103): clear all warp
   markers (Cmd+A, delete), find a downbeat by comparing with the original (the shouted "Hey"), set a
   warp marker there, Set 1.1.1 Here, delete stray markers, type the real BPM, metronome check. Use the
   first 4 bars of the chorus (the last 4 break differently), loop twice = 8 bars, trim a tail that
   sounds unnatural into the verse, then the original from the verse.
- Export only the edit track (Export Audio/Video, Rendered Track = that track).

## D17 Chris M, "How Pro DJs Keep The Dance Floor Full ALL NIGHT" (b2JvzT2sYhg, 09/01/2023, 7:16)
- Energy needs contrast: all-high = the main drop for an hour, exhausting; lows let the highs land.
  Energy isn't only hype: happiness, empathy, memories.
- Role in the line-up: warm-up never higher than the headliner; open low and rise slowly. Taking over
  from a DJ who went too hard: hold their level a while, lower it, lift again before handover. Taking
  over a sleepy room: match and rise, or late in the night the "water balloon" (jump straight to high,
  then ease off). Talk to promoters; arrive early and watch the crowd and the DJ before you.
- Building blocks: MAINTENANCE (hold), RISER (low -> high), COOL-DOWN (high -> slightly lower; a
  breakdown can do it), SHOCK (low -> high instantly; sparingly, surprise = dopamine).
- Tools: long intro-over-outro blends maintain energy; CUTS are the most powerful energy control (up
  AND down, e.g. cut into a breakdown after hard rinsing); loops and acapellas (a well-known vocal
  makes a medium track high-energy). Tag tracks by ENERGY LEVEL when importing (same BPM/genre ≠ same
  energy). Your own visible enjoyment transmits. Record your sets, map your own energy graph.

## D18 Crossfader, "How To Plan A DJ Set - (10 Ways To Do It)" (hjkTkb-_7mQ, 23/09/2021, 13:31)

## D19 Club Ready DJ School, "Why Your DJ Sets Lose Energy (make breakdowns count)" (qwkjG6Y5Yow, 23/06/2026, 16:20)
A long beatless breakdown can empty a floor (people go to the bar). Five fixes:
1. Mix the next track INTO the breakdown (skip the second half when it repeats the first, or bail out
   of a track that isn't landing).
2. Give them something to move to: bring a beat (another track's drums) and/or a vocal under/over the
   breakdown; bass on bass is fine when neither has bass there; can end in a double drop.
3. Mashup on the fly: a familiar acapella/spoken vocal over the breakdown (Beyoncé-type hook).
4. Loop part of the breakdown as a bridge into the next track.
5. Shorten it: jump (beat jump, in phrase) closer to the build-up; or echo out if unsure. Or play the
   breakdown over the OUTGOING track's outro beats so the melody of B rides A's beat, then B's drop.
- Don't kill every breakdown: keep the emotional, hands-in-the-air ones. "When you play a full
  breakdown, make sure it's worth it" (tension, emotion, anticipation); otherwise get to the good bit.
- Everyone mixing intro-over-outro sounds like 90 % of DJs.

## E03 James Hype, "Making A Remix - Start to Finish" (uUD_BO6gtQ8, 04/11/2023, 38:02; live stream)

## E08 Fred Dancekowski, "How to Make Mashups - An Ableton Live Tutorial (Part 1)" (pGfJ3eGMJ-w, 28/03/2011, 15:02)
Loop-based "sound collage" mashups (Girl Talk / DJ Earworm style):
- Cut loops at zero crossings, exact bar lengths (1, 2, 4 bars), starting on the downbeat; for a sound
  with silence at the downbeat (claps on 2 and 4), start the loop at the downbeat anyway; fill a missing
  reverb tail by pasting the tail over the loop start; draw short fades where there's no zero crossing
  (no clicks).
- Keep alternate endings (a bar with the transition/pickup) to bring a chorus in with variation.
- Beats and bass loops are easiest (clear attacks); vocals/riffs need their pickups.
- Always WAV, not MP3: MP3 decoding adds silence at the start/end and ruins exact loops (my measured
  12-34 ms mp3 offset in Live, 25/09/2026, is the same problem).

## E04 EDM Tips, "WARPING VOCALS in Ableton Live" (X74zABVIdHo, 05/05/2021, 12:24)
- Known vocal BPM: un-warp (auto-warp may have guessed), re-warp, type the vocal's real BPM into the
  clip's Seg. BPM, Complex Pro; drag so the FIRST TRANSIENT lands where wanted; or move the start
  marker (Cmd = off-grid), then right-click Set 1.1.1 Here so the grid lines up with the vocal.
- Tighten one syllable: hover a transient, Cmd shows neighbours, double-click to make warp markers
  EITHER SIDE of the word, then drag only that word onto the grid (without the side markers the whole
  vocal moves).
- Key: match like for like (minor with minor); count semitones on the piano roll (E minor vocal into
  B-flat minor = +6 or -6; pick the direction that sounds natural; formants can colour it).
- Unknown vocal BPM: solo it, metronome on, change the set tempo until the phrases sit (like beatmatching
  by ear), then warp at that BPM and return the set to the song's tempo.

## E07 DJ AndOne, "How To Make ReDrums" (A3u4ur2pNLM, 16/12/2020, 12:58)
Redrum of a rap track with plenty of low end but thin top: added only TOP-END drums (snare, hats, a
shaker-like "squeak", rim) + a small tempo increase for drive. Hats and shaker on Auto Pan synced
(e.g. amount 25 %, rate 1 bar) with one inverted so they bounce L/R opposite. Drum group: Glue Compressor
to gel, Utility gain down for headroom, Drum Buss (preset "drum sharpener", dry/wet brought up from 0),
parallel compression on a return (-25 dB send; threshold down, output up), sidechain the hats/perc to
the SNARE (ratio max, attack min, release ~50 ms) so the snare cuts through.

## E09 Loopcloud/The DJ Podcast, "Master Tempo Automation In Ableton Live" (CbjVZ20U6Os, 11/05/2012, 5:19)
- Main track automation lane "Song Tempo": set its min/max range fields (e.g. 125-130) so the lane has
  resolution. Two shapes: a STEP (instant jump, e.g. right after a track fades out, or instrumental ->
  a vocal entering with no beat under it: the listener won't notice) or a RAMP across the transition
  (e.g. 125 -> 130 over a 32-bar blend; many tiny changes, unnoticed). Over an hour: 128 -> 138-140.
