# Craft: mashups, wordplay, edits, transitions, effects, mixes

The musical rules. Ableton steps live in the other references; this file says WHAT sounds right and
WHEN. Built 25/09/2026 from 14 videos (craft-log.md) plus two research reports, after Evan called my
first MCP mashup "a car crash" (two vocals at once, acapella dropped mid-phrase).

## 0. The five checks before anything plays (a mashup or transition fails if any one fails)
1. **One lead vocal at a time.** Instrumental side = official instrumental or stems with the vocal
   removed. Never trust a "no words found" lyric scan: chopped/effected vocals are missed. Overlap two
   vocals only on purpose (a call-and-answer wordplay, a single word).
2. **Phrase start on phrase start.** Sections line up with sections: breakdown with breakdown, drop
   with drop, chorus over drop, verse over breakdown/groove. The incoming part starts on "the one" of
   a phrase (end of a 32-beat / 8-bar block), keeping any vocal pickup that sits before the bar.
3. **One bassline at a time.** Swap on a phrase boundary (usually the one of the next 8 or 16).
4. **Keys compatible.** Same Camelot number, ±1, or A/B switch. Shift a vocal no more than about
   ±2 semitones (formants on); split a bigger move between vocal and instrumental.
5. **Energy matches.** Don't drop one track while the other is mid-breakdown (Blakey). A mashup follows
   one energy arc: intro -> build -> drop -> breathe -> drop -> out.

## 1. Phrasing (C08 Blakey, C09 Shortee, C10 Chris M)
- Beat (letter) -> bar = 4 beats (word) -> phrase (sentence). Dance music: 8, 16, 32, 64-bar phrases,
  most often 8 bars = 32 beats with bigger changes at 16/32. Vocal music (pop, hip-hop, rock):
  2/4/8/16-bar phrases; verse typically 16 bars, chorus typically 8 (sometimes 4 or 16).
- Clues: small fill every 4 bars, crash every 8, a big change (new instrument, drum roll, riser,
  everything pulled back) at 16/32. Small clues mid-phrase, big clues at the end.
- Sections aren't always powers of two: a 24-bar breakdown = 16 + an 8-bar bridge. Count in 8s and
  find the biggest change. Intros are often 2 or 4 bars shorter/longer than the grid suggests, so the
  phrase grid can sit OFF bar 1 (both my test songs: 2 bars). Anchor on where the vocal lines start and
  where the bass enters, not on bar 1.
- Hip-hop/pop mixing: bring the next track in on a chorus and have the old one OUT before its next verse.
- Fixing misaligned phrases: move by whole phrases (beat jump ±32 beats = 8 bars) until breakdowns and
  drops coincide; extending a breakdown by 8 bars is fine.

## 2. Mashups (C01 Chris Deluxe + research)
- Choose: same key (or compatible), tempos close (see research numbers below); audition the pair
  first by ear.
- Parts: stems > official instrumental + acapella > separated (artifacts). Take the acapella's key and
  BPM from its ORIGINAL song; check an internet acapella's speed against the original (C04).
- Headroom: every channel about -6 dB before starting; gentle glue compressor on the Main at the end.
- Warp the acapella against its original (no kick in an acapella).
- Placement: acapella section start on an instrumental section start. Classic: chorus/hook over the
  drop, verse over the groove or breakdown. A few bars (8 counts) of instrumental space before the
  vocal enters builds tension. Bring the acapella in at the other song's break/build-up so everything
  lands together on the drop.
- Glue: short reverb/delay on the vocal, EQ carve so vocal and instrument don't fight, sidechain.
- FX quiet: risers/sweeps you barely notice; a sweep crossing both songs binds them.

## 3. Wordplay (C07 Trayze, C12 Crossfader + research)
- Wordplay = two songs linked by a word or phrase; the lyric is the transition. Works across genres,
  tempos and energy levels.
- Types: word-to-word ("California" in Around the World -> California Love), name-drop (a lyric names
  the next artist: "Mariah" -> Mariah Carey; "Tupac" -> 2Pac), question/answer, same phrase in both.
- Find them: listen to the words of the playing song, search the library for the same words (titles
  and lyrics).
- Build: loop the 1-2 bars holding the word so it calls out, then cut or 1-beat echo out on the beat
  and drop the next song on its one (or on its matching word). Finger-drum patterns on hot cues
  ("watch me, watch me, work it") on the one. Practise the pattern over a plain beat first.
- In Ableton: split out the word onto its own track (so the source song keeps playing clean), place it
  on the grid, echo/reverb throw on the last word, next song's downbeat right after.
- Finding pairs (Off The Rack Jack): note each track's opening words; search genius.com and read the
  LYRIC MATCHES list, Cmd+F the word to find which section it's in; keep a phone list of ideas heard
  while playing. Evan's library has lyric research already (Lexicon rebuild) worth searching too.
- Two ways to play it: LOOP FIRST (2-beat loop for a phrase, 1 beat for a single word; repeat it a
  few times, bring B in at full volume on its matching words, flick between the two every 2 beats so
  the words bounce, then stay on B) = impact; MIX FIRST (normal intro-over mix, then loop the word in
  A near the end and hand over on it) = subtle. Wordplay can sit in a verse, so the mix-in point moves
  to where the word is, not the usual end-of-chorus.

## 4. Edits: intros, outros, extended versions, DJ edits (C13 Crossfader, C14 Laidback Luke)
- DJ intro/outro = 16 bars (8 for fast sets): beats with a hint of the track, no vocal, no bassline
  (save the bass for the drop). Make the OUTRO first, reuse its beats for the INTRO with the opposite
  dynamic (fade/filter in, not out). EQ the low end out of the kick/bass in the last/first 8 bars "as
  if the DJ is EQing". Keep the outro in one key. Reverse cymbal + fill into the track proper.
- Watch loop artefacts: a copied 4-bar loop repeats its crash every 4 bars; crashes belong every 8.
- DJ edit: keep the magic, add "chunk": modest speed-up (e.g. 120 -> 125), club drums over the
  original bassline, extended build (filtered vocal loop, snare roll across the build, riser, crash on
  the one), each added element raising the energy.

## 5. Transitions and effects (C11 Club Ready, C12 Crossfader + research)
- EQ is the cake, FX are the icing. A clean EQ blend is the default; FX occasionally, not every mix.
- FX go on the EXITING track ("saying goodbye"): 1-beat echo out, spinback, trans/gate, noise sweep,
  low-pass crunch, vinyl brake.
- Drop swap: same BPM/key; cue both 32 beats before their drops; start B when A hits its cue; cut to
  B on the drop.
- Big tempo jump: loop an isolated vocal bit, gate/echo it, change tempo under the loop, drop the new
  track from 4 beats before its drop. Or echo out and start clean at the new tempo.
- Key-shift riser: 4-beat vocal loop stepped up in semitones for tension, back to the original key,
  drop on the one.
- Tempo across a set: automate the Song Tempo over a phrase or two (Chris Deluxe: 122 -> 126 over
  ~40 s); bigger jumps = cut or echo out.

## 6. Mixing numbers (research report 25/09/2026; sources in craft-log.md)
- Tempo: stay within about ±5% of a track's original BPM (Digital DJ Tips); meet in the middle
  (120 -> 126 mixed at 123); drift about 1 BPM per 30 s; bigger jumps: half/double time, a ramp
  during a build (128 -> 150 at 1 BPM per bar over 8 bars, Crossfader), or echo out / cut. Complex Pro,
  small stretches.
- Keys (Camelot): same number, ±1, or A<->B safe. +2 = "energy boost", use every 20-30 min, not always
  (Mixed In Key); +7 = one semitone up. Clashes don't matter over drums-only sections; they matter when
  vocals, synths or melodies overlap. Transpose ±1 st is usually clean, ±2 audible on vocals.
- EQ: never two basslines. Blend: highs over ~4 bars, mids over ~4, bass over a whole 8-16-bar
  phrase. EQ swap: highs cut at a 4-bar section, bass swapped exactly on the phrase change, mids at the
  next 4-bar section. Don't swap a heavy bass for a thin one (energy drops). EQ Three per track.
- Levels: channels peak about -6 dBFS; light bus compression 2-3 dB; limiter ceiling -1 dBTP. Upload
  target about -14 LUFS integrated (Mixcloud 2025, SoundCloud); club mixes often -9 to -10 LUFS by
  taste, then keep true peak <= -1 dBTP. Level-match tracks so the mix holds one volume.

## 7. Choosing the transition (decision rule)
| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| Same genre, compatible keys, similar energy | Long blend, 16-32 bars, EQ sequence |
| Pop/hip-hop, no DJ intro, crowd wants the hook | Cut on the one of a new phrase, into a chorus or drop |
| Keys clash, same BPM | Cut or filter (HPF out 8-16 bars) |
| Tempo or genre jump, end of a vocal line | Echo out on the last word (1/2 or 1 beat, feedback 50-70%, fader down, B on the next downbeat, echo low end cut) or reverb wash |
| Outgoing track ends too abruptly | Loop 4-16 beats of its drums, blend, release |
| Statement moment (peak, hard dance) | Spinback/brake, drop swap; once or twice a set |
| Linked lyrics | Wordplay (section 3) |
- FX restraint: "if a track sounded better with constant effects, the producer would've made it that
  way" (Digital DJ Pool). A couple of go-to effects known well beats many.
- Ableton: Echo (Sync, Dotted/Triplet, Feedback, HP/LP), Auto Filter DJ type (one bipolar control: +
  high-pass, - low-pass), Reverb wet 50-70% on the last word with lows/mids EQ'd out, riser across the
  last 4-8 bars high-passed, crash on beat 1. Filter sweeps sound better on an ease-in curve than a
  straight line.

## 8. Mix and set structure
- Energy arc: open at 60-70%, build, peak, release; don't burn the biggest tracks in the first 30 min.
  Recorded ~60 min mix: recognisable opener, 3-4 peaks with a breather between, key lifts (+2/+7) at
  peaks, an anthem to close. House ~20 tracks/hour; pop/hip-hop/open format 15-25/hour (2-4 min each);
  never cut before a chorus or drop.
- Weddings (Evan's bread and butter): instant hooks in the first bars; rotate eras; broad crowd-pleasers
  first, the couple's favourites later; dinner -> build -> peak in the final hour; never two slow songs in
  a row, follow a slow one with a medium "rebuilder"; open the floor with a "consensus bomb"; prepare
  entrance, first dance (short custom edits), parent dances, cake, last song.

## 9. Car-crash checklist (run before telling Evan anything is done)
Two basslines; two vocals; off-phrase entry; key clash over melody/vocal; too much FX; tempo stretched
beyond ~5%; heavy-to-thin bass swap; level jump between tracks. Measurements passing is necessary, not
sufficient: Evan's ears are the test.

## 10. Mashup numbers and method (research report 25/09/2026)
- One instrumental supplies ALL drums, bass and chords; the other song supplies only its vocal.
- Test the pair on its CHORUS first: loop the chorus over the instrumental's drop and the most exposed
  chord change; "if the central hook fails, the pair is probably wrong". A matching key label is not
  enough; the chord function can still feel wrong. Spoken/rapped vocals care far less about key.
- Tempo: within ~10% (5-10 BPM); project tempo between the originals, weighted about 2/3 toward the
  instrumental (its cymbals/transients smear first); a vocal takes only a few BPM of stretch cleanly.
  Half/double time pairs (70/140) share the pulse, not necessarily the phrase lengths.
- Pitch: 1 semitone = speed factor 1.059 (120 BPM down 2 st ~ 107 BPM if varispeed). Vocal ≤ 2 st with
  Complex Pro, Formants 100; shift the instrumental or split the move rather than push the vocal.
- PICKUPS: many lines start on the "and" of 4 or earlier. Put the warp/bar-1 on the STRESSED syllable
  and let the pickup hang before the bar; cut the acapella at phrase start MINUS the pickup. Never move
  the first word onto beat 1. Count 4/8/16 by ear.
- Section mapping default: chorus over drop, verse over breakdown/groove (then try alternatives).
  Pre-choruses and bridges are the hardest parts. Don't switch vocals too often.
- Sources: official > separated (lossless/320 k; dry centred pop/R&B vocals separate best; reverb and
  autotune leak). Solo the instrumental and listen for any vocal before arranging (my exact failure).
  HPF a separated vocal at ~80 Hz to remove kick bleed. Never pair stems from different versions.
- Arrangement template: tease 8 bars (instrumental + one vocal fragment) -> establish 16 (verse/hook,
  reduced layers) -> switch 8 (reveal the second source) -> payoff 16 (strongest vocal over strongest
  production) -> exit 8 (DJ outro); add 16-32-bar DJ intro/outro for club use. Quieter verse, bigger
  chorus: dynamics are the arc.
- Vocal mix: HPF 80-150 Hz; 1-2 cuts of ~2 dB in the instrumental's upper mids (ideally dynamic EQ
  keyed by the vocal); light compression (~3:1, fast attack, a few dB); automate the vocal level line
  by line; pop vocal ~2 LU under the instrumental (rock ~3); never bone-dry: short reverb, shared
  reverb/delay for glue; fades on every hard edit; limiter on the Main; check in mono; test in a set.
- Mistakes: picking by fame; trusting BPM tags; pickups moved to beat 1; key labels only; stretching
  too far; two low ends; keeping every part; mixing in solo; no arc; dry vocal; mismatched versions.

## 11. Wordplay and edit formats (research report)
- Wordplay edits often CHANGE TEMPO AT THE WORD: the word hides the cut, so tracks needn't be
  beatmatched or share a key (pool "segue" edits: "Single Ladies In Da Club" 97->91, "Uptown Funk /
  Just Wanna Love U" 115->100). Recognisable in 1-2 words, so famous hooks. DAW method: cut on the
  downbeat after the linking word, 1/4 or 1/8-beat echo on the word, start the next track on its phrase
  boundary with its word/pickup landing where the echo repeats fall; the echo tail covers a tempo jump.
- Live method (DJ Lawrence): outgoing vocal stem, 1-beat echo on the linking word; 4-beat loop of the
  incoming vocal; trade the two vocals on the faders over 16 bars; drop the stem, full track in.
- Edit formats: intro edit 8/16/32 bars (16 default; house needs 16-32 before a bass swap); outro 8-16
  (32 fine); template intro 8-16, hook 16-32, breakdown 8-16, main return 32, outro 8-16; redrum = more/
  heavier drums in 8-bar sections; hype intro = claps/acapella/riser build before the hook; BPM Supreme
  "Jump Off" (≥90 BPM): 8 intro -> 4-8 hype break -> chorus -> 8 outro, 1-2 min; slam edit = explosion
  before the hook for fast cuts/tempo changes; hook/chorus-first intro edits; quick edits 2-3 min;
  clean edits (swears muted/reversed). Why pools make them: a vocal-free stretch to lay over the
  outgoing chorus, because two vocals together "is very confusing".

## 12. Music theory that decides whether a pairing works (research report, round 2)
- A key is a pool of 7 notes, not a chord. Two "8B" songs can loop different progressions (I-V-vi-IV vs
  IV-I-V-vi) and rub. THE killer: a held vocal note a semitone from the instrumental's bass/chord root
  on beat 1. Passing notes on weak beats rarely matter. Chord function matters: a vocal resolving
  "home" while the instrumental sits on V (dominant) feels wrong without sounding dissonant.
- Relative keys (8A/8B) share notes but not the "home"; a minor-feeling vocal over a major loop can be
  the wrong emotion. Many "minor" pop songs loop vi-IV-I-V and pass for either.
- Fixes in order: different section (chorus-chorus easiest, verse harder, pre-chorus/bridge hardest;
  chord-level matching needed in ~25-50 % of mashups) -> shift the instrumental by 4/8 bars so another
  chord sits under the held note -> transpose the INSTRUMENTAL ±1-2 st -> use its breakdown (few chord
  changes = most forgiving bed) -> replace the bass with a pedal note or new bass following the vocal
  -> only then transpose the vocal (formants on).
- Key tags: Camelot number = Open Key + 7 (mod 12); B = d = major, A = m = minor. +7 on the wheel = +1
  semitone, +2 = +1 whole tone. Detection errors are usually a fifth (±1), relative or parallel key, i.e.
  one step on the wheel: treat an unverified tag as ±1. Tools agreed on only 39 % of 200 tracks (Crossfader
  12/2024); Mixed In Key is the most accurate common tool; all are weakest on hip-hop, modal, sparse or
  vocal-led tracks and on key changes.
- Key changes: ~1/4 of 1960s-90s #1s modulate (the +1/+2 "truck driver's" final chorus); almost none
  after 2010. For any pre-2005 pop/ballad acapella, CHECK THE LAST CHORUS. Options: cut before the lift,
  transpose that clip back down, or ride it (jump the instrumental up too as a planned lift).
- Maths: 1 st = ×1.0595 speed (+5.95 %); semitones = 12·log2(new BPM / old BPM); 128 -> 124 = -0.55 st.
  Re-Pitch (varispeed) links tempo and pitch; Complex Pro Formants 100 keeps the voice (lower Envelope
  for high voices, raise for low).
- Limits (practitioner): vocal ±1-2 st natural, ±3 with formants, beyond = effect; instrumental ±2-3 st
  fine (bass/sub smear first). A +1 st lift at the last chorus = the mashup truck-driver change; once.
- Swing: 50 % straight, 54 % loose, 66 % triplet swing. A vocal recorded over swung 90s/2000s hip-hop/R&B
  drags or flams over straight house 16ths: match the groove (Live's Groove Pool / extract groove from the
  original), choose similar-swing instrumentals, or chop and re-place phrases.
- Half/double time: 65<->130, 70<->140, 75<->150 are free. 90-100 BPM hip-hop does NOT double into house;
  pair it with 90-105 reggaeton/moombah/dancehall, or stretch only a hook, or use half-time sections.
  12/8 & 6/8 can sit on swung 4/4 (4 dotted beats per bar); true 3/4 waltzes don't fit 4/4.
- Anticipation: pop/R&B lines often land an 8th/16th BEFORE the downbeat; don't quantise it out.
- Rap: dense, carries its own groove, works over sparse beats in almost any key (melodic rap still
  needs key); dense rap over busy house tops clutters. House vocals: sparse on-grid chants.
- Arrangement: tension at the build, release at the drop (sometimes release into a stripped section
  instead); a hook needs space (no other lead melody/vocal); call-and-response by 2/4-bar phrases, not
  stacked, and not too often.

### Test-a-pairing procedure
1. Tags same or ±1 wheel (treat unverified as ±1). 2. Tempo within 10 % or exact half/double; note
straight vs swung. 3. Align the hook's first STRESSED syllable on bar 1, pickups before it. 4. Loop the
vocal's longest held note over the instrumental's most exposed chord change (usually the turnaround).
5. If it rubs: shift instrumental 4/8 bars -> transpose instrumental ±1-2 -> breakdown -> pedal/new bass
-> transpose vocal. 6. Check the acapella's final chorus for a key change. 7. 8-bar groove check
(swing, pushed lines, room for the hook). 8. Play the whole chorus at gig volume, not just a loop (ears
adapt to a loop in 30 s).

## 13. Open format, bars and Irish weddings (research report, round 2)
- Tempo journeys: DJ RINA shifts tempo every 15-20 min and pairs half/double (110<->55, 120<->60,
  130<->65); DJ Vice loops the outgoing track, widens pitch and rides it up, and plays a familiar EDM
  remix before heading back to hip-hop; A-Trak creeps tempo over several records or jumps with intro
  tracks; DJ Yoda uses a fat reverb at the end of a bar for big mood changes; "the later it gets, the
  slower it gets" (CC:DISCO!, 30-40 BPM across a night). Invisible drift: ~1 BPM per 30 s. Cut the highs
  when mixing across genres so hats don't clash.
- In open format, cuts, echo-outs and word cues do most of the work; keep bridge tracks in each BPM band
  and remixes of the same song in different genres (switch style while the crowd still knows the song).
- How long per track: club/event, let a song breathe "two verses, chorus, then make your move"; bar/pub,
  let most of it play; never cut mid-line; constant quick-mixing becomes "performative". Busy floor ~20-30
  tracks/hour, fewer in a bar.
- Programming: acts (steady -> rising tempo + some fillers -> peak with rehearsed "triads" of three
  bangers -> hold or wind down); several songs in a genre before switching; switch only when energy
  drops; tag by energy 1-5, mood, time of night. Warning signs: crossed arms, hips stop, talking; good:
  nodding, tapping, drifting to the floor. Requests can rescue a stuck floor; the couple's wishes beat
  guests'. Clean for weddings/corporate/daytime; bars: ask the manager (clean until ~10 pm).
- Jazzy Jeff: it's picking the right records "and how long to play those records"; the best DJ plays the
  unexpected record at the right time.
- IRISH WEDDING TIMELINE: band ~9 pm-midnight (two sets); DJ takes over 11 pm-midnight to ~1:30-2 am;
  first dance ~9:30-10 pm; late food ~11:30; finale ~1:45 am. Danger: the "midnight slump" (band packs up,
  tea and sandwiches). Handover: be live before the band's last note, zero silence, lights change, an MC
  line, then an "energy anchor" that bridges the band's style into the DJ's pace. Other slumps: after the
  ceremony, after dinner, room turnaround.
- Irish floor norms: Galway Girl (jive), Mr Brightside ("undisputed king"), AC/DC near the end (ties
  round heads), the farewell circle round the couple (The Parting Glass, World of Our Own, Don't Look
  Back in Anger, Mr Brightside). Crates like "Party Classics for Aunt Mary", 90s dance, 00s cheese, 80s,
  Irish, waltzes, slow set. Older guests usually stay through the band in Ireland.
- Multi-generation: rotate eras; hooks in the opening bars; 70s disco / 80s pop / 90s anthems unite
  ages; "three-song test" (invitation, commitment, confirmation). Slow songs ~1 per 5 fast, never several
  in a row; exit a slow song via one with a big climax/upbeat ending. First-dance edits: cut a verse,
  fade after a resolution (never mid-line); first-dance remix edits that jump into a party track.
- Genre pairs: hip-hop -> house = half/double time (64<->128), loop-and-ramp, or echo out and slam the
  house intro; house -> hip-hop = delay/reverb breakdown then drop at a break, or double-time; R&B ->
  house = stems + key shift, 104 -> 126 over 16 bars; into hard dance = ±16 % ramp with key lock off over
  the 8-bar build; into/out of slow songs = echo/reverb tail into the ballad intro, out via its climax.
- Recorded mix in Ableton: one track per song, Complex/Complex Pro, first downbeat on 1.1.1, blends 16-64
  bars for house (shorter for higher energy), volume as curves not straight lines, EQ bass trade at the
  swap point (most overlooked), glue seams with reverb/delay/filter/beat repeat, export offline.
- Mistakes: peak tracks too early; jumping 75 -> 128 in one go ("whiplash clears the floor"); too many
  slow songs in a row; a gap at the band handover; cutting mid-lyric; explicit versions at family gigs.

## 14. Energy and breakdowns (Chris M, Club Ready, Crossfader)
- Contrast makes energy: lows let the highs land. Blocks: maintain, rise, cool down, shock (sparingly).
  Cuts are the strongest energy control, up and down. A known vocal/acapella lifts a medium track.
  Tag energy 1-5 on import.
- Taking over: match the previous DJ's level, then steer; the warm-up never out-plays the headliner.
- Breakdowns: a long beatless one can empty the floor. Mix into it, put a beat or a familiar acapella
  over it, loop part of it as a bridge, shorten it by whole phrases, or lay it over the outgoing
  track's outro beats. Keep the emotional ones: a full breakdown must earn its time.
- Read a track for room: sparse drums+bass tracks invite acapellas and loops; busy tracks, leave alone.
  Remixes of the same song in other genres change the vibe while the crowd still knows it.

## 15. How the masters build mashups (primary interviews; research report round 2)
- Start from ONE song you love, then hunt an unexpected partner ("detective work"); aim for surprise and
  a smile, not same-genre pairs (Go Home Productions / Mark Vidler). Bootie: the "OMG WTF" factor.
- "The a cappella is the master tempo" (Max Tannone, Jaydiohead): keep the vocal at or near its own tempo
  and pitch; never chipmunk it. Danger Mouse measured every acapella's BPM first.
- DJ Earworm: stems/acapellas first (isolate if needed); every snippet its own file; ONE shared key
  chosen to "do the least damage" to most songs; tempo ≈ the average; ONE instrumental drives the whole
  thing; vocals kept close to their original key. Story: paste all lyrics in one document, find the
  thesis, build sentences from phrases, an emotional journey ("from darkness into light"); layer
  samples so texture and momentum grow; is it mixable, will it drive the energy?
- Re-cut the vocal to the groove "syllable by syllable" until it locks (A Plus D); build beds from
  small fragments (spare riffs, single hits) rather than looping whole sections (Danger Mouse,
  Tannone): "to make it all sound cohesive is the real battle".
- Number of songs: 2 for a surprise edit, 3-4 for a vibe (Kap Slap: three acapellas over one
  instrumental), many only for a theme megamix. Crowd knowledge: current/known songs keep them with you.
- Feels "written that way" = one key, one tempo, one driving instrumental, natural-pitch vocals, lines
  that answer each other, hook moments stacked late once momentum has built.
- Energy philosophies differ: Girl Talk starts at 100 and stays; Four Color Zack maps highs and lows and
  hides routine seams. Jazzy Jeff: "produce a DJ set like I would produce a record".
- Routines/wordplay: cue points do the work; keep it simple; "if it doesn't sound right, don't force it";
  draft lots, walk away, return with fresh ears (late-night ideas can be "prime turkeys" the next day);
  rehearse until air-tight (A-Trak), show friends, film yourself, cut what drags.
- Quality bar (A Plus D): "drop that mashup ANYWHERE and get an instant dance floor". Novelty alone has
  aged badly; avoid cliché/overplayed pairs and out-of-key tone play.

## 16. THE PLAYBOOK: how to make each of Evan's five things (use this order; every gate must pass)

### A. Mashup (acapella of song V over instrumental of song I)
1. Idea first: one sentence on the mood/story and why this pair (surprise, crossover, a lyric that
   means something new over this beat). Pick songs the room knows. 2 songs for an edit; 3-4 max.
2. Gate: tempo within ~10 % (or exact half/double); keys same or ±1 on the wheel (treat tags as ±1
   uncertain); rap vocals are key-tolerant, sung ones are not; check swing (swung 90s/2000s vocal over
   straight house = trouble).
3. Parts: official acapella/instrumental > stems. Instrumental must be VOCAL-FREE: solo it and listen
   end to end. Acapella key/BPM from its ORIGINAL song; check its speed against the original.
4. Project tempo: the instrumental's (or weighted 2/3 toward it); "the acapella is the master tempo"
   means don't push the vocal far: ≤ ~6 % stretch, ≤ 2 st pitch (formants 100), shift the
   instrumental instead.
5. Map sections: chorus over the instrumental's drop, verse over its groove/breakdown; the vocal's
   first STRESSED syllable on the bar line of an instrumental section start; pickups hang before
   the bar. Find both songs' real phrase grids (first vocal lines, bass entry, big changes), not bar 1.
6. Test on the chorus: loop the vocal's longest held note over the instrumental's most exposed chord
   change. If it rubs: shift the instrumental 4/8 bars -> transpose instrumental ±1-2 -> breakdown bed ->
   pedal/new bass -> transpose vocal. Check the acapella's last chorus for a key change.
7. Arrange the arc: tease 8 (instrumental + one vocal fragment) -> establish 16 -> switch/reveal 8 ->
   payoff 16 (best vocal over best section) -> exit 8; DJ intro/outro 16 if it's for the club. Re-cut the
   instrumental to follow the vocal song's structure (Heavy Hits); pick and choose vocal lines, don't
   wall-to-wall (James Hype); leave room before the hook (space = impact).
8. Mix: channels -6 dB; vocal HPF 80-150 Hz; instrumental upper mids -2 dB (or around 3 kHz, dynamic
   EQ keyed by the vocal); vocal comp ~3:1 light; short reverb/delay for glue (never bone-dry); pop vocal
   ~2 LU under the music; automate vocal level per line; fades on every cut; limiter on the Main,
   -1 dBTP.
9. Car-crash check (section 9), then play the whole chorus at gig volume, then Evan listens.

### B. Wordplay transition/edit (song A's word -> song B)
1. Find the link: A's last lines / B's first words; name-drops; question/answer; search Genius lyric
   matches; library lyric fields. Famous words only (recognisable in 1-2 words).
2. Choose the style: LOOP FIRST (impact: loop the word 1 beat, or the phrase 2 beats, repeat 2-4 times,
   B lands on its matching word at full volume, optional 2-beat call-and-answer flicks) or MIX FIRST
   (subtle: blend B's intro normally, loop A's word near the end, hand over on it).
3. Tempo can change AT the word (echo tail covers it): cut A on the downbeat after the word, 1/4 or 1/8
   echo (or 1-beat) on the word, B starts on its phrase boundary with its word/pickup where the echo
   repeats would fall. Keys matter little for the word itself; avoid A's melody playing under B.
4. In Ableton: split the word onto its own track, warp markers either side of it, Set 1.1.1 on B,
   echo/reverb throw automated on the word only.

### C. Transition in a mix (A -> B)
1. Decide what the moment needs (energy up, hold, down, genre/tempo change) BEFORE choosing the move.
2. Same genre/key/energy -> long blend on phrase boundaries (16-32 bars; highs 4 bars, mids 4, bass
   swapped on the phrase, never two basslines). Drop swap/double drop for peaks. Keys clash -> drums-
   only overlap or cut/filter. Tempo/genre jump -> echo out (1 beat) + B at a pre-chosen strong point, or
   a beatless break, or half/double time, or a ramp during a build (≤ ~5 % per track; Song Tempo
   automation). Pop/hip-hop -> in after A's chorus, A out before its next verse; cut on the one into a
   chorus/drop.
3. FX only on the exiting track, sparingly (the icing): echo out, reverb wash, HPF 8-16 bars, riser across
   the last 4-8 bars (high-passed), crash on the one.
4. Check: sections line up (breakdown with breakdown, drop with drop), no two vocals, no two basses.

### D. Bootleg / DJ edit / extended version
1. Name the problem the edit solves (no intro, too long breakdown, wrong energy, needs chunk).
2. Intro 16 bars (8 fast): beats + a hint, no vocal, no bassline; OUTRO first, then intro with the
   opposite dynamic; low end EQ'd out of the first/last 8 bars as if the DJ is EQing; crashes every 8, not
   every 4; reverse cymbal + fill into the track. Hype intro / chorus-first / slam variants per purpose.
3. Redrum/bootleg: keep the magic (original bass/synths/vocal), add what's missing (club drums, top-end
   percussion, sidechain), modest speed-up; extend builds (filtered hook, snare roll, riser, crash).
4. Keep outros in one key; render only the edit track; test in rekordbox before a gig.

### E. Recorded DJ mix
1. Plan the arc (opener 60-70 %, rises, cool-downs, 3-4 peaks, anthem close; +2 key lifts at peaks,
   sparingly); pick transitions per moment (C). Open format: 15-25 tracks/hour; a pop song gets at least
   verse + chorus unless a quick-mix section is the point.
2. Ableton: one track per song, Complex/Complex Pro, first downbeat 1.1.1, EQ Three per track, bass swaps
   on phrases, volume curves, Song Tempo automation for ramps, Utility trims to level-match, limiter on
   the Main (-1 dBTP), about -14 LUFS for uploads (louder only by choice). Export offline (Export
   Audio/Video), then listen back start to finish and map the energy.

## 17. Case study: my first MCP mashups (25/09/2026), judged by the playbook
Pair: Pumped Up Kicks (F minor 4A, 128) acapella over Coma Cat (A minor 8A, 119).
- v1 "car crash" (Evan): Coma Cat still had its vocal (fails A3); the acapella started mid-phrase at an
  arbitrary Coma bar (fails A5).
- v2 (bars 299-317): vocal-free Coma stems, chorus line 1 on Coma's drop after its 2-bar fill (A3, A5
  pass) but the PAIR fails A2 before any editing: 4A vs 8A are four steps apart, not neighbours; closing
  it needed +2 st on the vocal (the limit) and -2 on the instrumental, and I never ran the A6 held-note
  test. The right move was to choose a different partner for either song (a 4A/3A/5A/4B instrumental
  for the vocal), not to force this one.
- Lesson: most mashup failures are decided at SELECTION (tempo, key distance, structure, mood). Spend
  the effort there; editing can't rescue a wrong pair.
